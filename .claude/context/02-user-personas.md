# User Personas

## Primary Persona: Adam (System Consumer)

**Role:** Senior Product Designer — sole author and user of this design system
**Location:** London
**Tech comfort:** High. Comfortable with Figma, design systems, code, CI pipelines, MCP tooling.

### Who he is

Adam uses this design system to build and maintain the portfolio website. He is the designer, developer, and content author in one. The system needs to make his workflow fast, consistent, and maintainable — so he can focus on content and case studies rather than rebuilding UI from scratch.

### Goals

- Build and maintain the portfolio site efficiently as a sole practitioner
- Keep tokens, components, and site in sync with minimal friction
- Document the process as a case study demonstrating AI-assisted design system workflow
- Get the site live and attracting the right roles by November 2026

### What the system needs to give him

- A reliable Figma → GitHub pipeline so visual changes flow through without manual CSS work
- Documented components in both Figma and code so he always knows what's available
- Enough flexibility to refine content and visual direction without rebuilding

## Secondary Persona: Marcus

**Role:** Head of Product Design
**Age:** 40 · London (Shoreditch / Old Street)
**Company type:** Series C fintech or consumer tech, 150–500 people, design team of 5–10
**Devices:** MacBook Pro, iPhone
**Tech comfort:** High. Uses Figma, Notion, Linear daily.

### Who he is

Marcus is the main hiring decision-maker. He's looking for someone who can own a product area end-to-end — not just execute, but shape what gets built. He scans fast and only slows down when something earns it. He's not looking for the best visual designer; he's looking for strategic thinking, cross-functional influence, and sound judgment.

### What matters to him

- **Outcomes.** Numbers, even rough ones. Impact over activity.
- **Decisions.** Why that call? What was ruled out?
- **Seniority signals.** Ambiguity, stakeholder management, cross-functional ownership.
- **Quality without noise.** Considered design — but not trying too hard.

### How he browses

Lands on homepage, reads the intro, clicks one case study. If the opening paragraph doesn't hook him in 30 seconds, he's gone. Desktop reader, linear. Forwards a link if he thinks there's something there.

## Tertiary Persona: Priya

**Role:** Senior Product Designer (informal hiring partner)
**Age:** 31 · London (remote-first)
**Company type:** B2C consumer app or marketplace, Series A–B
**Devices:** MacBook Air, iPhone
**Tech comfort:** High. Lives in Figma. Comfortable with AI tooling.

### Who she is

Priya is often pulled into hiring conversations as a design eye. She evaluates craft closely — she'll zoom into the UI, check typographic consistency, notice whether spacing is intentional. She's also quietly asking: is this someone I'd want to work with?

### What matters to her

- **Craft.** The portfolio site itself is a design artefact — it will be judged as such.
- **Range.** Systems thinking and visual polish; process and craft.
- **Honesty.** Case studies that acknowledge constraints or failures feel more credible.
- **Point of view.** A consistent aesthetic sensibility, not a hired hand.

### How she browses

Clicks through multiple case studies. Looks at the full portfolio page for range. Zooms into UI details. Reads the about section. Desktop at work; screenshots things that impress her.

## Tertiary Persona: Tom

**Role:** Technical Recruiter (in-house or agency)
**Age:** 29 · London
**Company type:** Design/tech recruitment agency or in-house talent team at a scale-up
**Devices:** MacBook, iPhone — often browses quickly on mobile
**Tech comfort:** Moderate. Familiar with LinkedIn and ATS tools; not a design evaluator.

### Who he is

Tom is often the first gatekeeper — checking the portfolio before deciding whether to move forward. He's not evaluating design quality; he's doing a credibility check. He moves fast, has a mental checklist, and won't spend more than two minutes at this stage.

### What matters to him

- **Clarity.** What is this person, what level, what have they done?
- **Navigation.** Find things without thinking.
- **Recognisable brands.** giffgaff, Lickd, Burberry mean something.
- **Contact.** One tap to reach out.

### How he browses

Scans homepage, looks at case study list, clicks one project, skims the first scroll. Often on mobile. Needs the mobile experience to be as clear as desktop.

## Quaternary Persona: Yemi

**Role:** Founding Designer / Head of Design (first design hire)
**Age:** 34 · London (Soho / remote-first)
**Company type:** Early-stage startup, Seed–Series A, 10–40 people, no existing design team
**Devices:** MacBook Pro, iPhone
**Tech comfort:** Very high. Ships design and often writes basic front-end.

### Who she is

Yemi has been holding design together solo and finally has budget to hire. She wants someone who can operate at her level and take ownership without hand-holding. She identifies more with engineers than traditional designers — she'll look for systems thinking, technical credibility, and evidence of working fast under constraints.

### What matters to her

- **Ownership.** Did this person drive the work, or were they one of twelve?
- **Technical credibility.** Any sign they understand how things get built. Design systems work is a strong signal.
- **Small team experience.** HubbleHQ and early Lickd are highly relevant.
- **Speed and pragmatism.** Evidence of shipping something imperfect and iterating.

### How she browses

Skips the hero. Goes straight to case studies. Looks for messy real-world work: tight deadlines, limited resources, decisions made without full information. Often browses late at night; sends a LinkedIn message the same evening if something clicks.
